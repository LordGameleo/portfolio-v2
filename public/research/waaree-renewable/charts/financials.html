Waaree Renewable Technologies Ltd — financial dashboard, FY2018–FY2026

Nine financial years to March 2026, consolidated. ⚠️ READ THE FIRST FOUR YEARS AS PREHISTORY, NOT AS A TRACK RECORD. Revenue was ₹2 crore in FY18, ₹7 crore in FY19, ₹6 crore in FY20 and ₹13 crore in FY21; the company only became an operating business of scale in FY22, when revenue reached ₹162 crore. Every ratio struck on those first four years divides by a number close to zero and is arithmetically correct but economically meaningless — debt was 900% of sales in FY21 on ₹13 crore of sales and ₹117 crore of borrowings, which says almost nothing about the business that exists today. The series is shown in full rather than truncated, because hiding it would be a worse distortion than labelling it, but the medians and growth rates used in the valuation work on this page deliberately start at FY22. Return on capital is not plotted for FY18 because the source reports none for that year, so the line begins at FY19. Consolidated statements exist only from FY18; a twelve-year standalone series is available but covers the same near-dormant shell for its extra years and would add length without adding history. Borrowings are gross. Every metric on its own panel — the series span ₹ crore and percentages across a 100× range, so one shared axis would be meaningless.

    My notes

      Source: screener.in consolidated financials, fetched 20 September 2026, with the audited FY2026 annual report used to cross-check and to supply the segment split. Every plotted value comes from one dataset on one consistent definition for all nine years; the trailing twelve-month column is excluded because it is not a fiscal year. Two things need stating before any panel is read. First, the first four years are a pre-operational shell and their ratios should not be read as an operating record — see the note above. Second, this company buys a large and rapidly growing share of its materials from its own 74% parent, and the price at which it does so is not disclosed anywhere; because those purchases are roughly three-tenths of input cost, the reported margin is not fully independent of a related party. Return on equity is computed on year-end equity, not average, which understates it in a year when equity doubles. Borrowings are gross. Point-in-time data — not investment advice.
